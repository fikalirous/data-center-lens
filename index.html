The Cloud's Footprint

Mapping the world's data center infrastructure against the carbon, water, and land footprints of the electricity that powers it — grounded in UNU-INWEH's 2026 global assessment of AI's environmental cost.

About this project

In 2025, the world's data centers consumed an estimated 448 TWh of electricity — enough that if data centers were a country, they'd rank 11th globally by electricity use. AI workloads alone accounted for roughly a fifth of that, a share projected to double by 2030 as total data center demand climbs toward 945 TWh. But the environmental cost of that electricity isn't uniform: a kilowatt-hour drawn from a hydropower-heavy grid and one drawn from a coal-heavy grid carry very different carbon, water, and land footprints — and "low-carbon" doesn't automatically mean "low-water" or "low-land." This project maps where that infrastructure actually sits, and what powering it costs depending on where it's hosted.

Ireland: small country, outsized load. By 2023, data centers accounted for 21% of Ireland's total metered electricity, up from 5% in 2015 — exceeding the combined electricity use of all urban households. Dublin's grid operator paused new data center connection approvals in the region until 2028. (UNU-INWEH, 2026, p.35)
